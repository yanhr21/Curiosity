01

Physics — FPS vs. collision quality

What actually trades speed for penetration in Newton's hydroelastic contact. Details: context.md §3, experimental_conclusions.md.

The dials

What moves penetration — and what doesn't

  • XPBD: penetration ∝ 1/iterations, FPS ∝ 1/iterations — a genuine speed↔quality dial.
  • panda_hydro (MuJoCo + hydroelastic SDF): iterations saturates by ~5, ls_iterations does nothing, substeps/impratio only cost FPS.
  • The real lever is kh (hydroelastic stiffness) — but it trades against dt, not FPS.
The finding

kh is capped at ≈ 1/dt

  • MuJoCo's implicit integrator caps effective stiffness at ≈1/dt: at large dt, kh is inert; raise kh and substeps together to push penetration down (0.033 mm costs 25 FPS vs 73).
  • Cost is collision-bound (SDF queries), not solver-bound — sdf_max_resolution is the FPS lever.
  • Penetration metric (solver-agnostic): d = n·(bx_b−bx_a) − (m0+m1); pen = max(0,−d).
02

Tactile — sensing contact & material

How a robot feels: contact forces, dense pressure maps, shear, and material response. Design: tactile_pipeline.md.

Signals

SensorContact + hydroelastic surface

  • SensorContact (world frame): per-pad total force + friction; per-object force_matrix. Create before model.contacts().
  • Dense map: get_contact_surface() → per-face signed depth; pressure = kh·max(0,−depth) (verified sign law).
  • Pad-centric heatmap anchored to the fixed pad frame (gravity-up) so it doesn't balloon as contact slides.
Material model (verified)

Rigid share kh; soft → FEM

  • All rigid materials share a hard kh≈1e12; they differ only by friction, restitution, density — not hydroelastic stiffness.
  • Only genuinely deformable materials become FEM (VBD), differentiated by Young's E. VBD stiffness is iteration-limited, not E-limited (~50 iters for a straight rod).
  • Shear result: holding a light pen, the two pads shear it in an opposing couple (net≈0); pushing it into a cup, both pads shear downward together (~12–17 N) — measured on the pen, world frame.

04

Ingestion — datasets → Newton

The new framework: lift pre-arranged scene datasets into Newton physics + tactile. Design: dataset_ingestion.md; code: scene_ingest/.

Why. Generation makes one object at a time; the Explore stage needs whole human-arranged, contact-rich rooms. SAGE-10k already ships 10k rooms / 565k objects with mass, PBR, and placement baked in — far cheaper than generating scenes. One path lifts any such dataset into Newton.

Verified: the adapter parses a real SAGE room (layout_84b703fb) → 59 objects (3 static furniture), 4 walls, 1 door, support hierarchy floor 21 / on-table 11 / on-bookshelf 8 / wall 19. scene_ingest/adapters/sage.py is runnable stdlib; newton_build.py is a reference implementation to run once the newton env is up.

The point of ingestion isn't to reproduce SAGE's exact scene — it's to randomize the physics the demonstration never revealed (mass, friction, compliance), so the same intent demands a different solution. Same sink as generation; cheaper, richer, whole-scene.
