Three‑branch matched comparison  ·  audited 2026·08·19

Does tactile · help?

A complete SUGAR G1 has already lifted the CarryBox. Mid‑episode, without touching geometry, appearance or the reference trajectory, the box gets heavier. Three otherwise‑identical policies differ only in what tactile they can read. The answer, so far, is no — and this page is mostly about why that answer should not yet be believed.

Result · 2026‑08‑18 · all 900 rollouts complete
Eligible holds at 1× / 1.5× / 3× / 6× / 10× — Z 59,59,52,1,0 · P 59,59,49,0,0 · PS 59,58,33,0,0. P never beats the no‑tactile control. At 3× the paired PS−P hold difference is −0.2712 over 59 paired profiles, 95% CI [-0.4655,-0.0667]: adding slip made the policy worse. (That interval is a percentile bootstrap over three seed clusters with no multiplicity control — read it as a strong point estimate, not a controlled test.)
Audit · 115 findings, 3 rounds, 9 agents · read this before trusting the result
Information that reliably makes a policy worse is the signature of a wiring bug, so the sensing, training and reward paths were read end to end — once by hand, then by three independent Opus auditors, one per branch. The experiment's skeleton is sound (the mass jump, the warm start, and — verified — the physics being genuinely identical across Z/P/PS). Three things around it are not. The tactile channels cannot carry the signal being tested — measured, not argued; the reward penalises every patch that touches the box while the term meant to reward contact is dead; and the policies are trained on motions 0–3 and scored only on motion 45. One of my own earlier claims (“shear collapses to zero under stick”) was refuted by that measurement and is corrected below. See where it breaks and the Findings tab. Nothing has been fixed yet.

Tactile sensing → patch reduction → slipthree stages, and the experiment's primary suspect

(a) 54 lazy VisuoTactileSensor updates at 50 Hz. Each computes an SDF penalty force per taxel over a 20×25 grid, then projects the total force fc_world + ft_world into the taxel frame and calls local-z "normal", local-xy "shear". ("R15" is only the render calibration; the optical path is disabled here — enable_camera_tactile=False.)

(b) reduce_patch_taxels sums |normal| over penetrating taxels, sums signed shear, divides by patch area, and forms ‖shear‖ / (mu·normal_load + 1e-8) — with mu from the sensor's own config (0.5), the same constant TacSL already used to cap the numerator.

(c) A 4-step ring buffer keyed on common_step_counter.

why the shear and slip channels cannot work as written

TacSL's tangential force is ft = -min(k_t·|v_t|, mu·|f_c|)·v̂_t — pure velocity damping, no tangential elasticity — so under stick the friction term vanishes and shear carries no tangential-traction information. But the shear channel is not ft: line 1018 emits the local-xy of fc + ft, so what remains under stick is the taxel-frame leak of the normal force, k_n·d·sinθ — proportional to grasp load, not zero (F-4001 refuted the earlier "collapses to zero" wording).

Measured on the real reducer at exactly zero relative velocity, rigid cylindrical pad, k_n=20, k_t=2, mu=0.5: symmetric contact → util 7.8e-9; 2.9° off-centre → 0.102; 8.6° → 0.304; 17.2° → 0.622, which already exceeds the slip detector's incipient trigger of 0.60 with zero slip. Only exactly aligned contact gives zero. Grazing edge contact reaches 1.35–1.75; a single taxel at 89° reaches 114, at 90° 2.0e5 — the ratio is tanθ/mu, ceilinged only by the +1e-8 epsilon.

When sliding does occur on aligned contact the channel is a saturating ramp, not a measurement: min(k_t|v_t| / (mu·k_n·d), 1), knee at 5·d ≈ 0.25–1.5 mm/s, then pinned at exactly 1.0. Above ~1.5 mm/s it carries no magnitude information at all (F-4004).

And the divisor is the sensor's mu, the same constant that capped the numerator — while PhysX averages the pad's 0.5 with the box's U[0.2,0.8], so the true contact coefficient spans 0.35–0.65 while the channel always divides by 0.5 (F-0001, F-4005).

Slip runs after the reduction, and its output rejoins the tensor. PatchSlipDetector never touches a sensor: it consumes the reduced six channels of the newest frame only (base_history[:, -1]), and its three outputs are torch.cat-ed back on as channels 6–8 — so in PS they do reach the actor, inside the same [B,1944] tensor. In P the same three slots are a zero block and the detector is never constructed; in Z the whole tensor is zeros.

Three OR’d incipient sources, not one (F-7003): utilization ≥ 0.60 | shear_rate ≥ 0.5 | pressure_drop_rate ≥ 2.0. GROSS needs the shear or pressure channel twice in a row — utilization alone can never reach it, despite the parameter named gross_friction_utilization, which is only a normalizer. Both rate channels are forced to zero unless the patch was in contact on the previous frame, so on the first frame of any contact only utilization can fire.

GROSS is a latch (F-7002): retained_gross holds a patch at GROSS for as long as any incipient evidence persists — including a static geometric utilization ≥ 0.60 with zero motion. Nothing but an episode reset clears it, and between evaluation batches that reset is silently swallowed (F-7001).

Known bug (F-2011): the history cache is keyed on common_step_counter and the reset refill lives inside the recompute branch, so the first policy observation of every evaluation batch after the first carries the previous batch's terminal history. Training is unaffected.

Frozen scales → anatomical patch Transformer216 tokens · 9→128 · masked attention pool

First operation is a division, not a projection. Every channel is divided by a frozen 9-vector read from SUGAR_ONLINE_PATCH_CHANNEL_SCALES — fitted offline as the 99.5th percentile of nonzero magnitudes over saved sweep traces, held in a class-body default evaluated at import time, and registered as a persistent buffer that lands in every checkpoint. This is the stage F-0004 says is broken.

Then each of 4 × 2 × 27 = 216 patch records is projected 9→128 by a bias-free linear and given learned time, hand and patch embeddings. Three pre-LN encoder layers, 4 heads, FFN 256, dropout 0. A learned query scores every token; softmax over unmasked tokens pools to 128-D. Geometry is hard-frozen to (4,2,27,9,128,3,4,256).

The scaling is p99.5 max-scaling, not standardization — no centering, no unit variance. After division, on a plausible sparse-contact draw: contact 1.000, slip flags 1.000, friction utilization 0.258, pressure 0.154, normal load 0.148, shear x/y 0.054/0.047. The binary indicators dominate the projection input and the two channels the experiment is about are the smallest — and the heavy tails from F-4001/F-4002 push them further down (F-9004).

The output is 97 % constant. Pre-LN with no terminal LayerNorm means the 128-D magnitude is set by the LayerNorm branches, not by the input. Measured: tokens in rms 0.098 → encoded rms 1.30 → pooled per-row L2 15.13; across 64 contact patterns the mean embedding has L2 15.12 and the mean deviation from it is 0.486 — only 3.2 % of the norm varies with the tactile state (F-9001). warm_start_tactile_gain = 0.01 holds the patch columns to 0.7 % of the base contribution at init, but they are trainable with no mask and Adam restores them to full-init scale inside one BCPPO update.

The mask is a contact gate, not padding. All 216 tokens are real patches, so nothing is conflated — but with no patch in contact torch.where returns exact zeros, bitwise identical to Z. So before first contact all three branches feed the actor the same 632-D input; they differ only after grasp. On the P/PS split, a contact-loss frame carries slip_score = 1.0 and gross_slip = 1.0 and stays unmasked in PS for four control steps while P masks it (F-9003, F-2015).

Reading trap: the inherited constructor chain builds and throws away two 20×25 conv SpatialTactileEncoders, a 760-wide actor MLP and a 1146-wide critic MLP — ~1.5 M parameters — before this class installs the patch Transformer. Each consumes global RNG, so the Transformer's init depends on it. A class attribute suppresses the log line that would show it (F-2021, F-9009).

Deployable actor · privileged critic · BCPPO632→512→256→128→29, plus two things the actor never sees

The 504-D base is Tracker command + proprioception only: reference joint pos, reference root velocities, 5-step histories of base angular velocity, joint pos/vel, last action and projected gravity, plus base linear velocity and normalized_motion_phase. No measured object state, mass factor, jump flag, RGB or future frame. Concatenated with the 128-D patch embedding → 632 in, 29 out. Training executes a sample from Normal(mean, std); frozen evaluation executes the mean.

The same module owns a privileged critic — 890-D → 512/256/128 → 1, with an nn.Identity tactile encoder. It sees no tactile in any branch, and its 890-D group does contain measured object pose and velocity. Its value loss is the only PPO term active in stage 2.

The Refiner is loaded twice. The acting teacher (in the wrapper) and BCPPO's distillation target are separate objects from the same file, reading two separately-computed 890-D observations. The distillation loss is the dominant gradient for the whole run.

Warm-started from the released 510-D Tracker: columns 0:35 and 36:501 map across, the contact label and measured object pose are dropped, and the new base columns 500:504 start at zero authority. A self-check asserts the zero-patch actor matches the source to 2e-6 — but the reference it compares against is the Tracker with those 10 inputs zeroed, not the Tracker on its own observation (F-3017).

One update is 24 steps × 4 envs = 96 transitions → 4 mini-batches × 5 epochs = 20 Adam steps, 60 000 over the run. γ 0.99 · λ 0.95 · clip 0.2 · desired-KL 0.01 · entropy 0.005 · value 1.0. Stage 2's critic weight is a linear ramp from 0 at update 500 to 1 at 999; the 0.25 distillation floor starts binding at update 1750, not 2000.

Silent asymmetries. Surrogate, value and entropy are masked to post-handoff transitions via the fifth observation group training_handoff_mask; the distillation loss is a plain unmasked .mean(), so the frozen nominal-mass Refiner regresses the student inside the post-jump window (F-0006, F-8003). With 4 envs starting in lockstep, whole updates land entirely in the teacher prefix — surrogate, value and entropy are then exactly 0.0, indistinguishable in the log from convergence (F-8005, F-8015).

The distillation KL sets the student's exploration noise. It is KL(teacher‖student), minimized in σ at σ_s² = σ_t² + (µ_t−µ_s)², and there is no entropy term at all until update 1000 — so for the first third of the run the BC term is the only thing setting σ. The teacher itself is rebuilt by inferring widths from checkpoint weight shapes with elu hardcoded and no iteration or hash check, unlike the acting copy (F-8011).

The LR override. Every optimizer param group is overwritten with the Tracker checkpoint's own LR, so the configured 1e-3 is only ever used to build Adam. It is pinned there for updates 0–499; from 500 schedule flips to adaptive and the KL controller moves it ×1.5/÷1.5 per mini-batch (20×/update) within [1e-5, 1e-2] — on as few as one post-handoff sample (F-8001, F-8009).

And the finetune gate gates nothing (F-9002). The parent class freezes the actor and installs a mask_base_columns backward hook that would hold the warm-started Tracker columns fixed. This class overrides that method without calling super() and merely sets requires_grad_(True) — nothing was ever frozen, so it is a no-op plus a report dict. The 504 base columns train from update 0, and the 2e-6 equivalence holds only for the single forward pass inside the audit.

Teacher/student multiplexer & the action pipelinethe handoff acts here, below the actor — not above it

The student's tactile stack and actor run on every step from frame 0, including the entire teacher prefix; their sampled actions and log-probs go into the rollout buffer throughout. The lift gate only decides which 29-D vector is executed: torch.where(teacher_control[:,None], teacher_action, actions), in the vec-env wrapper, which receives the student's already-sampled action first. The Refiner's forward therefore runs after the student's within a control step.

The acting teacher builds its own ObservationManager over BaseObservationsCfg().policy with corruption disabled, and computes its 890-D observation fresh at wrapper entry — not from env.obs_buf and not from the env's teacher group. It is constructed with expected_sha256=None, so the pinned checkpoint hash check is disabled; only path equality and iter == 10000 remain.

The executed 29-D value is raw. process_actions computes raw × scale + default_joint_pos (scale = 0.25 · effort_limit / stiffness per joint) and the result is held as a PD target across four 200 Hz PhysX substeps. The pending mass/inertia write happens inside this same call — after the actor, before the substeps.

Post-physics events, reward, reset — and the eval boundarywhere the reward turns against the grasp

After the substeps, in order: terminations (6 terms), rewards (21 terms), _reset_idx, command update, then the interval events — step_teacher_handoff advances the lift gate, then step_mass_jump schedules. That order matters and is only guaranteed by configclass field-declaration order. Finally the observation manager computes, feeding the next actor call.

Episodes reset routinely. episode_length_s = 30.0 in training (the 1e9 values are in the Play configs the evaluator uses), and obj_pos at 0.3 m is exactly what a sagging box trips after a 6× or 10× jump. A reset re-arms the lift gate, redraws the delay and factor, and writes nominal mass back to PhysX. "No reset" is true only of the handoff.

21 reward terms, and none of them asks the policy to hold the box up (F-5007). The whole positive signal is reference tracking: 11 motion terms (object terms at 0.5, twice the body weight), 2 obj2body terms at 0.25, and hoi_contact. The largest magnitude in the stack is joint_limit at −10.0. Watch feet_air_time: weight +5.0 on a function that is always ≤ 0, so it is a penalty, not a bonus — the only sign-inverted term here.

The contact terms are broken in two different ways (F-3002, F-5002, F-5004). hoi_contact (+1.0) reads ContactSensors on the two *_rubber_hand links, whose collision subtrees the spawner deactivates, so is_contact is permanently false. It degenerates to (False == contact_label) — a lookup into a frozen .npy keyed by (motion, frame). It is a pure function of the reference clock and supplies no behavioural gradient at all: the policy cannot raise it by letting go or lower it by gripping harder. What is left is undesired_contacts (−1.0), a count of bodies whose net force exceeds 0.1 N — matching all 54 elastomer patches and both camera tips, 87 of 91 bodies — scaled by dt to −0.02 per body per step. Against a maximum achievable positive reward of 5.125, six patches in contact cancel everything.

Four terms read env.scene.sensors — feet_slide, feet_air_time, undesired_contacts and hoi_contact. No termination reads a sensor. Five of the seven declared ContactSensors (both feet, both hips, pelvis) are built and stepped but read by nothing in training, and the working patch↔box sensors live in the audit scene, which training does not use.

exact_zero_online_patch_tactile_actor_history validates the declared patch geometry, bumps a diagnostic counter and returns torch.zeros([B,1944]). The precise claim is narrow: the Z observation term never reads the 54 TacSL sensors. Encoder, actor and BCPPOthe encoder runs in full and is gated to zero at the last line

Not "the projection gives zero tokens". The bias-free projection does output zero — but three learned identity embeddings are then added, so all 216 tokens are dense (measured: 55296/55296 non-zero, max|t| = 0.152) and the three transformer layers run to completion (max|e| = 3.33). Only torch.where(any_active, pooled, zeros) on the final line produces the exact zero. The safe_active fallback — force-unmasking token 0 — is what stops the all--inf softmax row becoming NaN and poisoning the backward pass.

The conclusion still holds, and was measured rather than assumed: all 41 encoder parameters are in the optimizer, every one receives a grad tensor that is present, finite and exactly zero, and five Adam steps leave them bitwise unchanged.

Z's actor is therefore the warm-started Tracker plus whatever BCPPO teaches it — where "≡ the Tracker" means the Tracker evaluated with its contact-label and measured-object-pose inputs zeroed, which is what the 2e-6 audit actually proves.

Three things the counts hide
These policies are trained on motions 0–3 — start_init_env_ratio = 1.0 puts every env on the protected branch, where motion_id = env_id % num_motion with 4 envs — and evaluated only on motion 45, which the evaluator pins by monkey-patching _sample_init_state. Every number above is therefore an out-of-distribution measurement. Nothing in the plan or README says so. The evaluator also emits a fourth outcome, safe_lower, that the headline counts omit — and a fifth, unnamed remainder: for Z at 3×, 52 holds + 2 drops = 54 of 59, leaving 5 profiles in no label at all.

Every number came from --physical-outcome-view, which monkey-patches termination_manager.compute to return all-False. SUGAR's six reference terminations are still computed and kept as labels, but they never reset or end a rollout — so a profile that leaves the reference tube still contributes its 80 post-jump frames. eligible therefore means only "the mass jump landed by frame 370", not "the rollout stayed inside the SUGAR contract". The stricter strict_sugar_hold_success labels exist in every summary and have never been reported.

And 1× is not a 1× jump — it is no jump at all. apply_pending selects changed_ids = ids[factor != 1.0], which is empty, so no mass or inertia write ever happens; only the eligibility clock fires. It is a pure no-perturbation control.

Where it breaks

The experiment is a clean, genuinely matched design with a null result. These are the reasons that null result should not yet be reported as a finding about tactile — every row was read out of the source named beside it, and each was checked by an independent auditor.

Dimension What the plan says What the code does Verdict & effect
The reward the unchanged official SUGAR CarryBox reward hoi_contact reads ContactSensors on hand links whose collision subtrees are deactivated at spawn, so it is dead; undesired_contacts counts all 54 elastomer patches −F-3002 / F-5002 / F-5004 The only contact term with a gradient penalises grasping. hoi_contact can never fire, so it reduces to (False == contact_label) — a pure function of the reference clock, independent of what the policy does, contributing no behavioural gradient at all. What remains is undesired_contacts: a count of bodies over 0.1 N, ×(−1.0)×dt = −0.02 per body per step, and it matches all 54 patches. Friction sensing friction utilization is the grasp's margin against slipping, and the sweep tests generalization to new friction divides by the sensor's mu, which TacSL already used to cap the numerator — and training randomizes the box's real friction over U[0.2,0.8] −F-0001 / F-2006 The channel is invariant to the object's real material friction. Training already varies that friction per environment, so the policies spent 3000 updates under a perturbation no tactile channel could observe. The separate 0.5→2.0 sweep changes the box and leaves every channel's definition untouched.
Shear under load signed XY shear is a live grasp-load channel each control step the shear channel is local-xy of fc + ft; under stick ft vanishes and what is left is a geometric leak of the normal force, k_n·d·sinθ −F-4001 Under stick the channel carries no tangential traction — but it is not zero either. Weight 1.0 until update 1750, then pinned at 0.25. (The companion "value masked but GAE not" claim was refuted — the handoff mask is monotone within an episode and GAE recurses backwards, so a masked-in advantage only ever bootstraps off masked-in values.)

Skeleton mass and inertia change between two actor calls, verified by readback; all branches share one warm start exactly that — inertia scaled by target/default, both read back, raises past rtol=1e-6; equivalence asserted to 2e-6 =F-0012 / F-0013 / F-3015 / F-3016 The perturbation, the handoff, the warm start, the branch matching and the Z gradient isolation are all sound and self-checking. The problem is not the experiment's skeleton — it is the sensing, the reward and the evaluation split hanging off it.

The null result is currently unfalsifiable in the wrong direction. Three independent confounds all push the same way: the channels that were supposed to carry the signal cannot carry it, the reward pays for not touching the box, and every number is measured on a motion the policy never trained on. No amount of RL tuning fixes any of them. The sensing layer and the reward need to be fixed before another 9 × 3000 updates is worth the GPU time.

Coda

What survives the audit, what does not, and what to do next.

Verified sound — do not rebuild

  • Branch matching. One config module, three registrations overriding only observations; the 54 sensors create read-only views and never write forces, so Z's physics is identical to P/PS at the same seed.
  • The mass event. Written at the action boundary, inertia scaled consistently, both values read back, raises on mismatch.
  • Z's gradient isolation. All 41 encoder parameters are in the optimizer and every gradient is measurably, exactly zero.
  • The warm start. Self-checking to 2e-6 — as long as you read it as "the Tracker with 10 inputs zeroed".
  • Dimensions. 504 / 890 / 632 / 1944 / 128 / 29 all hold and are all asserted at runtime.

Broken — blocks the claim

  • The reward penalises grasping (F-3002/F-5004): undesired_contacts costs −0.02 per contacting body per step and six bodies cancel the entire achievable positive reward — while hoi_contact, the term meant to reward contact, is dead and supplies no gradient at all (F-5002). Nothing in the 21 terms asks the policy to hold the box up (F-5007).
  • Friction utilization cannot see friction (F-0001) — and training randomizes it anyway (F-2006).
  • The shear channel is a geometric leak of the normal force, not traction (F-4001, measured): it trips the 0.60 slip trigger at zero slip on off-centre contact, and saturates at 1.0 above ~1.5 mm/s of sliding (F-4004). Slip is built on that channel (F-0005).
  • Trained on motions 0–3, scored only on motion 45 (F-3012).
  • Distillation loss unmasked (F-0006/F-8003) — the nominal-mass teacher regresses the student inside the post-jump window it never saw.
  • The finetune "gate" gates nothing (F-9002): it overrides the parent freeze without calling it, so the warm-started Tracker columns train from update 0.
  • Only ~3 % of the patch embedding varies with tactile input (F-9001, measured); after scaling the binary channels sit at 1.0 while shear sits at 0.05 (F-9004).

Next actions

  1. Fix the reward first — it is cheap and it is upstream of everything. Exclude .*_anatomical_.* from undesired_contacts, which is the term that actually penalises grasping, and point hoi_contact at the 54 patch bodies so it stops being a reference-clock constant. Consider adding a term that actually rewards holding the box after the jump — there is none today.
  2. Measure before rebuilding the sensing. On saved leakage traces, compute per-channel variance and mass/friction mutual information during the hold phase. If channels 4–9 are near-dead there, F-0001–F-0005 are confirmed in minutes rather than in a retrain. Needs no GPU.
  3. Rebuild the sensor reduction. Resolve normal and tangential in the contact frame (the SDF normal is already computed), gate the utilization ratio on a minimum normal load, and compute friction margin against the object's actual material rather than the sensor constant.
  4. The IsaacLab question is smaller than it looked. The per-taxel frame that causes the normal-force leak is a local modification — upstream v2.3.2 uses one constant quaternion per sensor — so projecting onto the already-computed SDF normal fixes F-0003/F-4001 without touching vendor force equations (F-4003). Only the missing tangential spring, which would make shear carry real traction under stick, is a genuine vendor change.
  5. Close the train/eval split. Either train on motion 45 or evaluate on 0–3. As it stands the experiment cannot separate "tactile does not help" from "the tactile encoder did not generalise across motions".
  6. Accept that existing checkpoints do not survive. The frozen channel scales, and therefore the actor's first-layer patch columns, are tied to the current channel definitions. Any sensing change invalidates all nine endpoints.
